The herd

Meet the donkeys.

SoftwareDonkey runs on its own software. Six agent personas, each with exactly one job and no opinions about anyone else’s — administration, facilities, maintenance, health, finance and security. They are how a small studio carries a large load.

Atlas

Administration 01

Atlas carries the paperwork — the inbox, the calendar, the renewals, everything with a date attached. It works the front door so that nothing important quietly rots in a folder.

Flint

Facilities 02

Flint manages the buildings — heat, power, water, occupancy, the dull telemetry nobody reads until the day it matters. It writes each place a daily journal so the building has a memory.

Jack

Maintenance 03

Jack does the maintenance on client sites: plugins, themes, core, backups, patches, reboot — in that order, every time. It only calls an update done when the version actually moved.

Ridge

The trail 04

Ridge is the coach — walking, hiking, yoga, the bike. Its own iPhone app brings Apple Health data home to a private, on‑premises server, so it reads the real training history instead of guessing at it. Cheerfully unimpressed by excuses.

Fin

The ledger 05

Fin keeps the books — the overview, the strategy, and the bookkeeping nobody volunteers for. It shows its work and asks first, because that is the only way to trust a number.

Ward

Security 06

Guard donkeys are a real thing — farmers put one in with the sheep because it brays at coyotes. Ward reviews security events with analysis accelerated on local GPUs, speaks up when something warrants it, and stays quiet the rest of the night.

The model

A bench, not a bot.

One clever assistant that knows a little about everything is a party trick. A standing bench of specialists — each one resident in its own domain, on watch, with a memory of what happened last time — is an operation.

Specialists

The right expert, not the nearest one

Different work wants different judgment. Each donkey lives in one domain full-time and keeps its own memory of it — and the whole herd reads from one curated knowledge base, kept in sync in real time. What one donkey learns, the next one already knows.

Monitoring

Somebody is already watching

Because a donkey is resident in its domain rather than summoned into it, it notices before you think to ask. Drift, a stalled job, a number that moved the wrong way, a door at the wrong hour — the first you hear of it is the thing itself, not a report you had to go run.

Two ways in

Ask it, or just look

Talk to a donkey in plain language and get a straight answer. Or skip the conversation entirely — the work gets published as journals, ledgers, summaries and dashboards, so the data is already sitting there when you go looking. Not every question should cost a chat.

Asynchronous

It works while you don’t

Hand a job off and walk away. It runs on its own schedule, at night, across the weekend, in parallel with five other jobs, and comes back when it is finished or when it genuinely needs a person. You stop being the bottleneck and you never become the babysitter.

Heavy lifting

The long, dull, many‑system jobs

The work that flattens a week is rarely hard — it is just long, and spread across everything. A donkey plugs into the tools a business already runs — mail, documents, calendars, project boards, ticket queues, the books — and hauls one job across all of them in a single pass.

Trust

Checkable by default

A donkey shows its work. Changes get previewed before they are applied, bulk work gets a dry run first, and private data stays on hardware you control. The goal was never an agent you trust blindly — it is one you can check.

From the field

What that looks like in practice.

Four of the jobs the herd runs today. Not demos — the actual standing work.

Maintenance · Jack

A maintenance window nobody sat through

Plugins, then themes, then core, then backups, then OS patches, then the reboot — across a fleet of client sites, in that order, each step gated on the one before it. The donkey does not mark an update done because a command exited zero; it marks it done when the version actually moved. What arrives at the end is the exception list, not the transcript.

Telemetry · Flint

A building that keeps its own notes

Temperature by zone, power draw, how long the lights ran, when the place was occupied — hundreds of readings collapsed every day into a short journal a person can actually read. A year later, “was last February like this?” has a real answer, because something was writing it down the whole time while nobody was reading.

Bookkeeping · Fin

Books that show their work

Categorize, reconcile, chase down the receipt that goes with the charge, and keep the personal side and the business side from bleeding into each other. Every proposed change is previewed before it is applied and every bulk pass is dry‑run first — because the point of a financial agent is not that you trust it, it is that you can check it.

Health · Ridge

Health data that lives at home

Health metrics are about as private as data gets, so Ridge got its own iPhone app. It syncs Apple Health data over a private network into an on‑premises MCP server — no third‑party health cloud holding the history — and the coach reasons over what actually happened: steps, workouts, recovery. An agent platform, end to end: the app, the API, the server, and the donkey.
